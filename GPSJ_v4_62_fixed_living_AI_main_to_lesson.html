Industrial Robot Anatomy

Understand how the major parts of an industrial robot work together before simulation or supervised real-world interaction.

1. What is an industrial robot?

An industrial robot is a programmable mechanical system designed to position and move tools or materials through a defined workspace. Most systems are built from a chain of rigid links connected by joints.

Core idea: the controller decides what motion is required; drives provide controlled power; joints and links create movement; sensors report what actually happened.

2. Major components

  • Base: anchors the robot and supports the structure.
  • Links and joints: form the mechanical arm and determine the robot's degrees of freedom.
  • Actuators/drives: generate motion at each axis.
  • Sensors: measure position, motion, force or proximity and feed information back to the control system.
  • End effector: the tool attached to the robot wrist, such as a gripper or task-specific instrument.
  • Controller: executes the programmed motion logic and coordinates the system.

3. Why feedback matters

Without feedback, the controller only knows what it commanded. Feedback allows it to compare the target with the measured state and correct motion. This is the basis of closed-loop control.

Safety principle: simulation and read-only observation come before supervised testing. Real-world control is only available when the user's role, authorization, equipment state and local requirements allow it.
